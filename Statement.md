Project Statement

Money has a strange way of disappearing without us noticing. One day, we feel like we have enough, and the next day we’re wondering where it all went — a snack here, a bus ride there, an online order we forgot about. For many students and everyday users, keeping track of daily spending isn’t just difficult, it’s something we rarely think about until it becomes a problem.

That’s where the idea for this project came from. I wanted to build something simple — nothing complicated, nothing fancy — just a small tool that helps record expenses and make sense of them later. The Personal Expense Tracker is a basic Python program that runs in the terminal and stores user input in a CSV file. Instead of relying on memory or manually writing expenses on paper (which most of us forget to do), this tracker gives a quick and organized way to record how much was spent, on what, and when.

The project includes features such as adding expenses with details like category, amount, and a short description. There’s also an option to view everything recorded so far, calculate the total money spent, and filter expenses based on a particular category like food, travel, bills, and so on. Since the data is saved in a CSV file, users can also open it later with Excel or Google Sheets if they want to analyze it further.

The main purpose of this project is to encourage better awareness of spending habits. Even though it’s simple, it can help users become more mindful about where their money goes and potentially make better financial decisions. In the future, this project can be improved with visual graphs, monthly breakdowns, a login system, or even a user-friendly graphical interface.

For now, this version serves as a small but useful tool — a starting point for learning, building discipline, and making personal finance feel a little less chaotic.
